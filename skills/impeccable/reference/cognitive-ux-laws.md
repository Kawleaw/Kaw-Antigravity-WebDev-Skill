# Cognitive UX & Human Perception Laws (Designer-Skills)

Expert cognitive ergonomics and perception-grounded UI/UX guidelines to ensure websites feel effortless and natural to human perception.

## 1. Key Laws of Human Perception
- **Fitts's Law**: Touch/click targets must be proportional in size to importance and distance (minimum 44x44px for primary actions, placed within thumb/cursor paths).
- **Hick's Law**: Decision time increases logarithmically with the number and complexity of choices. Group, chunk, and progressive-disclose options.
- **Miller's Law**: Working memory holds 7 ± 2 chunks. Never present unchunked lists or sprawling options.
- **Jakob's Law**: Users spend most of their time on other sites. Follow standard web navigation and affordance conventions unless there is an overwhelming UX reason not to.
- **Doherty Threshold**: Productivity soars when a computer and its users interact at a pace (<400ms feedback loop) where neither has to wait on the other.

## 2. Gestalt Grouping Laws
- **Law of Proximity**: Related items belong closer together than unrelated items.
- **Law of Similarity**: Elements with the same visual characteristics (color, shape, size) are perceived as having the same function.
- **Law of Common Region**: Enclose related elements in a distinct card/surface background.
- **Law of Closure**: The human mind completes partial shapes; use subtle indicators instead of heavy redundant boxes.
